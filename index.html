About

I work on the dynamics and control of systems that move on their own, from drones to spacecraft, and on the physics and numerical methods underneath them. My current research is at Seoul National University, reproducing SkyJEPA for autonomous flight.

My training ran through a French classe préparatoire, a year in the United States in aerospace and modern physics, and a specialization in materials and processes before the move into autonomous robotics. Across all of it, two questions stayed constant: how objects move in space, and the physics that governs them.

The projects below are where I turn that into working code. Each one is derived from first principles, tested against analytical references, and documented so the results can be reproduced.

02

Research

Two research directions, bridged by mathematics and scientific computing. The work spans orbital dynamics and control on one side, and the physics and numerics underneath on the other.

Space engineering & autonomy

Orbital mechanics, guidance, navigation and control, and robots that operate autonomously in orbit. The scope runs the full chain: from a clean two-body orbit to holding a satellite's attitude when sensors are noisy and the model is uncertain.

  • Astrodynamics
  • GNC
  • Space robotics

Theoretical & computational physics

Classical and Hamiltonian mechanics, modern physics, and the numerical methods that make them computable. The emphasis is on rigour: tracking invariants, checking convergence, and treating every simulation as a model rather than a measurement.

  • Mechanics
  • Relativity
  • Scientific computing

SkyJEPA

Research in progress

Reproduction of SkyJEPA for autonomous flight at SNU, covering 6-DoF dynamics, world models, and predictive control. The current objective is a faithful, diagnosable reproduction with documented robustness tests before any claim of a new result.

  • Autonomous flight
  • World models
  • Predictive control

Repository and project page to be linked once published.

OrbitLab

Planned

A computational astrodynamics study of one question: how faithfully can gravitational dynamics be integrated, and what does accuracy cost in conservation of invariants and compute time? It starts with the two-body problem, then the restricted three-body problem, comparing RK4 against symplectic integrators.

  • Astrodynamics
  • Numerical methods
  • Python

Educational MVP in planning. Repository to come.
