About

I care about one thing: being able to defend my own work. Not just run the code, but derive the equations behind it, say where the model breaks, and reproduce the number tomorrow. That is the bar I hold myself to, and it is why this site shows what I can actually do rather than a list of course titles.

The path here was not a straight line. A French classe préparatoire, a year in the United States on aerospace and modern physics, then a turn through materials and processes. Two interests kept pulling at me the whole way: how things move in space, and the physics underneath it. I am now building real depth in both.

Right now my days go to the lab, where I am reproducing SkyJEPA for autonomous flight. Everything else on this site is built in the hours around that work.

02

Research

Two directions I am working toward. I am not claiming to have arrived at either one yet. Math and scientific computing are what let me cross between them.

Space engineering & autonomy

Orbital mechanics, guidance, navigation and control, and robots that operate on their own in orbit. I want to work the full chain, from a clean two-body orbit up to holding a satellite's attitude when the sensors lie and the model is wrong.

  • Astrodynamics
  • GNC
  • Space robotics

Theoretical & computational physics

Classical and Hamiltonian mechanics, modern physics, and the numerical methods that make them computable. What draws me in is the discipline of it: watch the invariants, check convergence, and never mistake a simulation for a measurement.

  • Mechanics
  • Relativity
  • Scientific computing

Projects

Every project lives in its own repository, which is the real record. A project only moves forward once I can derive its equations, explain every assumption, and reproduce the results without help. The labels below are honest about where each one stands today.

SkyJEPA

Research in progress

My main lab work: reproducing SkyJEPA for autonomous flight, covering 6-DoF dynamics, world models, and predictive control. The goal for now is a faithful reproduction I can diagnose and stress-test. I will not call anything a contribution until it earns it.

  • Autonomous flight
  • World models
  • Predictive control

Repository and project page to be linked once published.

OrbitLab

Planned

My first project outside the lab. The question I want to answer: how faithfully can you integrate gravity, and what does accuracy really cost you in conservation and compute? Two-body first, then the restricted three-body problem, comparing RK4 against symplectic integrators.

  • Astrodynamics
  • Numerical methods
  • Python

Educational MVP in planning. Repository to come.
